With population data, we seldom know the Fractional Birth Rate and Fractional Death Rate, but we can often use historical data to calculate the Fractional Net Growth Rate. In addition, it turns out that the Fractional Net Growth Rate is the difference between the Fractional Birth Rate and the Fractional Death Rate.

Effect_of_Deer_Density_on_Predator_Births

GRAPH(Deer_Density) Points: (0.000, -1.000), (0.200, -0.950), (0.400, -0.900), (0.600, -0.800), (0.800, -0.500), (1.000, 0.000), (1.200, 0.500), (1.400, 0.800), (1.600, 0.900), (1.800, 0.950), (2.000, 1.000)

dmnl

This is a dimensionless value used to represent how the Deer Density on the Kaibab Plateau affects the growth rates of the predator herd. This variable is used as a multiplier to increase or decrease the Net Predator Growth Rate, according to the Deer Density value (with greater density implying more food for predators, and hence a greater reproductive rate). This Effect of Deer Density on Predator Births variable is also determined by a graphical relationship with Deer Density. When the Deer density is 0, the Effect is -1.0, meaning that the Predator population will experience negative growth (from a lack of food). When the Deer density is 1, the effect is 0, meaning that the Predator Population will experience flat growth (neither growing nor shrinking). When the Deer Density is more than 2 , the Effect of Deer Density on Predator Births is 1.5, meaning that the Predator Population will grow at 1.5 times the Baseline Fractional Net Growth Rate (because there are abundant deer available for food).

Effect_of_Deer_Density_on_Predator_Efficiency

GRAPH(Deer_Density) Points: (0.000, 0.000), (0.200, 0.050), (0.400, 0.150), (0.600, 0.350), (0.800, 0.700), (1.000, 1.000), (1.200, 1.200), (1.400, 1.300), (1.600, 1.400), (1.800, 1.450), (2.000, 1.500)

dmnl

This is a dimensionless value used as a multiplier to increase or decrease the Deer Predation, according to the Deer Density level. This value varies between 0 and 1.5. Its value is 0 when there are no deer present (i.e. when deer density = 0 deer per 1000 acres) and it is 1.5 when there is an abundance of deer (i.e. when the deer density reaches 10 deer per 1000 acres or more). Hence, if there are no deer present, the Deer predation will fall to zero. If there is an overabundance of deer, the Deer Predation will be 1.5 times as great as it would be in equilibrium. This variable was formatted in the diagram to use a boldface, underline font to highlight that its value is not set by an equation, but by a graphical function showing how the Effect is calculated based on the Deer Density.

Kaibab_Adequacy_fo_Forage

1-Ratio_of_Forage_to_Forage_Carrying_Capacity

dmnl

This variable is calculated as 1 – (Ratio of Forage to Forage Carrying Capacity). When the Biomass is well below the Carrying Capacity, the Adequacy of the Ecosystem to Support Continued Growth value will be close to 1. As the Biomass approaches the Carrying Capacity, thus the Adequacy of the Ecosystem to Support Continued Growth will approach zero. Higher Adequacy of the Ecosystem to Support Continued Growth values imply that the Biomass can grow at a more rapid pace.

Kaibab_Adequacy_to_Support_the_Deer_Population

1-Ratio_of_the_Deer_Population_to_Carrying_Capacity

dmnl

This variable is calculated as 1 – (Ratio of Population to Carrying Capacity). When the Population is well below the Carrying Capacity (and the Ratio of Population to Carrying Capacity is hence close to zero), the Adequacy of the Ecosystem to Support Continued Growth value will be close to 1. As the Population approaches the Carrying Capacity (and the Ratio of Population to Carrying Capacity hence approaches 1), thus the Adequacy of the Ecosystem to Support Continued Growth will approach zero. Higher Adequacy of the Ecosystem to Support Continued Growth values imply that the Population can grow at a more rapid pace. It has units that are dimensionless or a fraction.

Ratio_of_Forage_to_Forage_Carrying_Capacity

Forage_Biomass/Kaibab_Forage_Carrying_Capacity

dmnl

This is calculated as the ratio of the Forage Biomass to the Carrying capacity. This Ratio is less than 1 when the current Forage Biomass is less than the Carrying Capacity. In this case, the Forage Biomass still has “room to grow” in the ecosystem. However, as the Forage Biomass nears the Carrying Capacity, the Ratio of Forage Biomass to Carrying Capacity will get close to 1. As this happens, the Fractional Net Growth Rate should slow, moving toward a value of zero.

Ratio_of_the_Deer_Population_to_Carrying_Capacity

Deer_Population//Kaibab_Deer_Carrying_Capacity

dmnl

This is calculated as the ratio of the Population to the Carrying capacity. This Ratio is less than 1 when the current Population size is less than the Carrying Capacity. In this case, the Population still has “room to grow” in the ecosystem. However, as the Population nears the Carrying Capacity, the Ratio of Population to Carrying Capacity will get close to 1. As this happens, the Fractional Net Growth Rate should slow, moving toward a value of zero.
