In addition, it turns out that the Fractional Net Growth Rate is the difference between the Fractional Birth Rate and the Fractional Death Rate.Deer_Predation = Baseline_Annual_Kills_Per_Predator*Predator_Population*Effect_of_Deer_Density_on_Predator_Efficiency    UNITS: Deer/Years    DOCUMENT: The number of animals killed by predators per year.Forage_Consumed_by_Deer = Actual_Consumption_per_Deer*Deer_Population    UNITS: metric ton/Years    DOCUMENT: Change in the forage biomass on the Kaibab via consumption by animals.Net_Annual_Forage_Growth = Actual_Biomass_Growth_Fraction* Forage_Biomass    UNITS: metric ton/Years    DOCUMENT: Net change in the forage biomass on the Kaibab via natural growth and decay, apart from consumption by animals.Net_Predator_Growth_Rate = Predator_Population*Baseline_Fractional_Predator_Growth_Rate*Effect_of_Deer_Density_on_Predator_Births    UNITS: Predators/Years    DOCUMENT: The net number of predators added to (or removed from) the Predator Population each year through the natural birth and death processes. This value is calculated as the product of the Predator Population, the Baseline Fractional Predator Growth Rate, and the Effect of Deer Density on Predator Births.Predators_Hunted = STEP(Predator_Population*Fraction_Predators_Killed_per_Year, Program_Start_Year)    UNITS: Predators/Years    DOCUMENT: Number of predators removed each year through hunting.Actual_Biomass_Growth_Fraction = Baseline_Biomass_Growth_Fraction*Kaibab_Adequacy_fo_Forage    UNITS: 1/Year    DOCUMENT: This represents the actual Fractional Net Growth Rate of the Biomass, after accounting for the Carrying Capacity of the EcosystemActual_Consumption_per_Deer = Desired_Consumption_per_Deer*Effect_of_Forage_Availability_on_Consumption    UNITS: metric ton/deer/year    DOCUMENT: The actual amount of forage biomass consumed per deer.Actual_Fractional_Net_Growth_Rate = Baseline_Fractional_Deer_Growth_Rate*Kaibab_Adequacy_to_Support_the_Deer_Population    UNITS: 1/Year    DOCUMENT: This represents the actual Fractional Net Growth Rate of the Population, after accounting for the Adequacy of the Ecosystem to Support Growth of the population. It is calculated by multiplying the Baseline Fractional Net Growth rate times the Adequacy of the Ecosystem to Support Continued Growth. This, in effect, scales down the Baseline Fractional Growth Rate, according to how close the Population is to the Carrying Capacity.Baseline_Annual_Kills_Per_Predator = 40    UNITS: Deer/(Predators*Year)    DOCUMENT: The number of deer that would be killed per predator each year, assuming that there was a natural balance between the predator and deer populations. Baseline_Biomass_Growth_Fraction = 0.10    UNITS: 1/Year    DOCUMENT: This variable stands for the fractional net growth rate of the Biomass whenever there are no restrictions of the carrying capacity.Baseline_Fractional_Deer_Growth_Rate = 1.0    UNITS: 1/Year    DOCUMENT: This variable stands for the fractional net growth rate of the Population whenever there are abundant resources on which to live.Baseline_Fractional_Predator_Growth_Rate = 0.5    UNITS: 1/Year    DOCUMENT: This represents the fraction by which the Predator Population will grow each year, assuming there are an abundance of deer available for food. Deer_Carrying_Capacity_in_Years = 2    UNITS: Years    DOCUMENT: Number of years of forage needed to sustain the deer population.Deer_Density = (Deer_Population//Kaibab_Area_in_Thousands_of_Acres)/Normal_Deer_Density_per_Acre    UNITS: dmnl    DOCUMENT: The ratio of deer to area all of which is divided by a normal.  Results in a scale where one is normal and anything above 1 indicates that you have too many deer and anything below one indicates that you have to few deer compared to what is normal deer per acres.Desired_Consumption_per_Deer = 0.75    UNITS: metric ton/deer/year    DOCUMENT: The amount of forage consumed by a single deer in a year, when abundant forage is available.Effect_of_Deer_Density_on_Predator_Births = GRAPH(Deer_Density)Points: (0.000, -1.000), (0.200, -0.950), (0.400, -0.900), (0.600, -0.800), (0.800, -0.500), (1.000, 0.000), (1.200, 0.500), (1.400, 0.800), (1.600, 0.900), (1.800, 0.950), (2.000, 1.000)    UNITS: dmnl    DOCUMENT: This is a dimensionless value used to represent how the Deer Density on the Kaibab Plateau affects the growth rates of the predator herd. This variable is used as a multiplier to increase or decrease the Net Predator Growth Rate, according to the Deer Density value (with greater density implying more food for predators, and hence a greater reproductive rate). This Effect  of Deer Density on Predator Births variable is also determined by a graphical relationship with Deer Density. When the Deer density is 0, the Effect is -1.0, meaning that the Predator population will experience negative growth (from a lack of food). When the Deer density is 1, the effect is 0, meaning that the Predator Population will experience flat growth (neither growing nor shrinking). When the Deer Density is more than 2 , the Effect of Deer Density on Predator Births is 1.5, meaning that the Predator Population will grow at 1.5 times the Baseline Fractional Net Growth Rate (because there are abundant deer available for food).	Effect_of_Deer_Density_on_Predator_Efficiency = GRAPH(Deer_Density)Points: (0.000, 0.000), (0.200, 0.050), (0.400, 0.150), (0.600, 0.350), (0.800, 0.700), (1.000, 1.000), (1.200, 1.200), (1.400, 1.300), (1.600, 1.400), (1.800, 1.450), (2.000, 1.500)    UNITS: dmnl    DOCUMENT: This is a dimensionless value used as a multiplier to increase or decrease the Deer Predation, according to the Deer Density level. This value varies between 0 and 1.5. Its value is 0 when there are no deer present (i.e. when deer density = 0 deer per 1000 acres) and it is 1.5 when there is an abundance of deer (i.e. when the deer density reaches 10 deer per 1000 acres or more). Hence, if there are no deer present, the Deer predation will fall to zero. If there is an overabundance of deer, the Deer Predation will be 1.5 times as great as it would be in equilibrium.  This variable was formatted in the diagram to use a boldface, underline font to highlight that its value is not set by an equation, but by a graphical function showing how the Effect is calculated based on the Deer Density.Effect_of_Forage_Availability_on_Consumption = GRAPH(Ratio_of_Actual_to_Required_Biomass)Points: (0.000, 0.000), (0.100, 0.590), (0.200, 0.840), (0.300, 0.930), (0.400, 0.970), (0.500, 0.990), (0.600, 0.9957), (0.700, 0.9983), (0.800, 0.9994), (0.900, 0.9998), (1.000, 1.000)    UNITS: dmnl    DOCUMENT: This nonlinear or graphical function captures how Actual Consumption per Deer will change given the Ratio of Actual to Required Biomass. Deer will then start to eat less as foraging becomes more difficult.Fraction_Predators_Killed_per_Year = 0    UNITS: 1/Year    DOCUMENT: Fraction of predators killed each year.Historical_Deer_Behavior_Over_Time = GRAPH(TIME)Points: (1900.00, 4000.0), (1902.50, 4000.0), (1905.00, 4000.0), (1907.50, 5000.0), (1910.00, 9000.0), (1912.50, 15000.0), (1915.00, 25000.0), (1917.50, 35000.0), (1920.00, 65000.0), (1922.50, 90000.0), (1925.00, 60000.0), (1927.50, 37000.0), (1930.00, 25000.0), (1932.50, 20000.0), (1935.00, 18000.0), (1937.50, 12000.0), (1940.00, 10000.0), (1942.50, 7000.0), (1945.00, 5000.0), (1947.50, 3000.0), (1950.00, 2000.0)    UNITS: Deer    DOCUMENT: A reference mode showing the actual deer population estimates from 1900 through 1950. You can display this BOTG with the Deer Population to help us tune the model parameters to match the historical record.Initial_Deer_Population = 4000    UNITS: Deer    DOCUMENT: This is an exogenous variable that can be used to set (and change) the initial size of the Deer Population.Initial_Predator_Population = 100    UNITS: Predators    DOCUMENT: Used to set the initial size of the Predator Population.  Notice that this is different from the original predator-prey model you built.Kaibab_Adequacy_fo_Forage = 1-Ratio_of_Forage_to_Forage_Carrying_Capacity    UNITS: dmnl    DOCUMENT: This variable is calculated as 1 – (Ratio of Forage  to Forage Carrying Capacity). When the Biomass is well below the Carrying Capacity, the Adequacy of the Ecosystem to Support Continued Growth value will be close to 1. As the Biomass approaches the Carrying Capacity, thus the Adequacy of  the Ecosystem to Support Continued Growth will approach zero. Higher Adequacy of the Ecosystem to Support Continued Growth values imply that the Biomass can grow at a more rapid pace.Kaibab_Adequacy_to_Support_the_Deer_Population = 1-Ratio_of_the_Deer_Population_to_Carrying_Capacity    UNITS: dmnl    DOCUMENT: This variable is calculated as 1 – (Ratio of Population to Carrying Capacity). When the Population is well below the Carrying Capacity (and the Ratio of Population to Carrying Capacity is hence close to zero), the Adequacy of the Ecosystem to Support Continued Growth value will be close to 1. As the Population approaches the Carrying Capacity (and the Ratio of Population to Carrying Capacity hence approaches 1), thus the Adequacy of  the Ecosystem to Support Continued Growth will approach zero. Higher Adequacy of the Ecosystem to Support Continued Growth values imply that the Population can grow at a more rapid pace. It has units that are dimensionless or a fraction.Kaibab_Area_in_Thousands_of_Acres = 800    UNITS: K acres    DOCUMENT: Land area of the Kaibab Plateau.  This is approximately 800,000 acres.  We are measuring this in thousands of acres so that is why we use 800 when setting the value.Kaibab_Deer_Carrying_Capacity = Forage_Biomass/(  Desired_Consumption_per_Deer*Deer_Carrying_Capacity_in_Years )    UNITS: Deer    DOCUMENT: This is the population size that the ecosystem can sustain for the long term.Kaibab_Forage_Carrying_Capacity = 350000    UNITS: metric ton    DOCUMENT: Amount of forage biomass that the Kaibab can support on an ongoing basis.Normal_Deer_Density_per_Acre = 5    UNITS: Deer/K acres    DOCUMENT: The number of deer per acre under ideal conditions. Program_Start_Year = 1907    UNITS: year    DOCUMENT: The year the predator hunting policy is implemented. Values below 1 indicate a shortage of forge biomass.Ratio_of_Forage_to_Forage_Carrying_Capacity = Forage_Biomass/Kaibab_Forage_Carrying_Capacity    UNITS: dmnl    DOCUMENT: This is calculated as the ratio of the Forage Biomass to the Carrying capacity. This Ratio is less than 1 when the current Forage Biomass is less than the Carrying Capacity. In this case, the Forage Biomass still has “room to grow” in the ecosystem.  However, as the Forage Biomass nears the Carrying Capacity, the Ratio of Forage Biomass to Carrying Capacity will get close to 1. As this happens, the Fractional Net Growth Rate should slow, moving toward a value of zero.Ratio_of_the_Deer_Population_to_Carrying_Capacity = Deer_Population//Kaibab_Deer_Carrying_Capacity    UNITS: dmnl    DOCUMENT: This is calculated as the ratio of the Population to the Carrying capacity. However, as the Population nears the Carrying Capacity, the Ratio of Population to Carrying Capacity will get close to 1. As this happens, the Fractional Net Growth Rate should slow, moving toward a value of zero.Required_Forage_Biomass = (Deer_Population*Desired_Consumption_per_Deer) *Deer_Carrying_Capacity_in_Years    UNITS: metric ton    DOCUMENT: Forage biomass needed to sustain the existing deer population for the number of years indicated by the value of the Deer Carrying Capacity in Years.
